Musculoskeletal system components
Bones
oste/o, oss/i, osse/o
Structural framework; 206 bones in adults
Soft tissues
Muscles (my/o)
Produce movement and maintain posture
Tendons (tendin/o)
Connect muscle to bone
Ligaments (syndesm/o)
Connect bone to bone at joints
Fascia (fasci/o)
Connective tissue wrapping
Joints & cartilage
Joints (arthr/o)
Articulations between bones
Cartilage (chondr/o)
Flexible connective tissue at joint surfaces
5 bone shapes
Long bones Short bones Flat bones Irregular bones Sesamoid bones
Sesamoid bones form within tendons (e.g. the patella)

Cancellous bone
Spongy inner layer; lighter weight

Endosteum
Inner membrane lining marrow cavity

3 muscle types
Skeletal
Voluntary; attached to bone; striated; produces movement
Smooth
Involuntary; found in hollow organs (intestines, blood vessels)
Cardiac
Involuntary; striated; found only in the heart (myocardium)
Antagonistic muscles: work in opposing pairs — flexors vs. extensors.
Synergistic muscles: work together to produce the same movement.
Tendon origin: attachment point on the stationary bone.
Tendon insertion: attachment point on the moving bone.
14 muscle actions — paired opposites
Flexion / Extension
FlexionDecrease angle at joint
ExtensionIncrease angle at joint
Abduction / Adduction
AbductionMove away from midline
AdductionMove toward midline
Supination / Pronation
SupinationPalm turned upward
PronationPalm turned downward
Dorsiflexion / Plantar flexion
DorsiflexionFoot toes upward
Plantar flexionFoot toes downward
Eversion / Inversion
EversionSole turns outward
InversionSole turns inward
Protraction / Retraction
ProtractionMove forward
RetractionMove backward
Rotation / Circumduction
RotationPivot around an axis
CircumductionCone-shaped circular motion
Joint types (by range of motion)
Synarthroses
Immovable joints; e.g. skull sutures
Amphiarthroses
Slightly movable; e.g. vertebral joints
Diarthroses
Freely movable synovial joints; most common type
Bursa (burs/o) — fluid-filled sac that reduces friction at joints; pl. bursae.
Meniscus — fibrocartilage pad within synovial joints (e.g. knee); pl. menisci.
Osteoporosis
Loss of bone density; bones become porous and fragile. Common in postmenopausal women.
Osteomyelitis
Infection of bone and bone marrow (myel/o = marrow)
Osteomalacia
Softening of bones (-malacia) due to vitamin D deficiency; adult form of rickets
Rickets
Osteomalacia in children; bowing of weight-bearing bones
Osteopenia
Below-normal bone density; precursor to osteoporosis
Osteitis deformans
Paget disease; chronic bone disease causing enlarged, deformed bones
Chondromalacia
Softening of cartilage (chondr/o); often at the knee
Costochondritis
Inflammation of cartilage (cost/o = rib) connecting ribs to sternum
Osteoarthritis (OA) / DJD
Degenerative joint disease; wear-and-tear breakdown of joint cartilage
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)
Autoimmune inflammation of joint synovium; bilateral, symmetric pattern
Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS)
Compression of median nerve through the carpal tunnel of the wrist
Bunion
Bony protrusion at the base of the big toe (hallux valgus deformity)
Bursitis
Inflammation of a bursa; commonly at shoulder, elbow, or knee
Tendinitis
Inflammation of a tendon; often from overuse
Gnathalgia
Pain in the jaw; often associated with TMJ disorder
Scleroderma / SLE
Autoimmune connective tissue diseases affecting joints and other organs
Baker cyst
Fluid-filled cyst behind the knee joint (popliteal cyst)
Crepitus
Crackling or grating sound/sensation in joints during movement
Scoliosis
Lateral (sideways) S- or C-shaped curvature of the spine
Kyphosis
Exaggerated convex curve of the thoracic spine ("hunchback")
Lordosis
Exaggerated concave curve of the lumbar spine ("swayback")
Herniated intervertebral disk
Rupture of disk with nucleus pulposus protruding; may compress spinal nerves
Spondylolisthesis
Forward slipping of one vertebra over another (-listhesis = slipping)
Ankylosing spondylitis
Inflammatory arthritis of the spine causing fusion of vertebrae
Spinal stenosis
Narrowing of the spinal canal; compresses spinal cord or nerves
Lumbago / Dorsalgia
Lower back pain (lumb/o = lumbar); general back pain (dors/o)
Fibromyalgia
Widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, and tenderness; no structural cause
Myasthenia gravis
Autoimmune disease causing progressive muscle weakness ("grave muscle weakness")
Rhabdomyolysis
Breakdown of skeletal muscle (rhabdomy/o); releases myoglobin into bloodstream
Plantar fasciitis
Inflammation of fascia on the sole of the foot (plant/o + fasci/o)
Contracture
Permanent shortening of a muscle or joint from fibrosis or scarring
Polymyositis
Inflammation of multiple muscles (poly- = many)
Muscular dystrophy
Genetic disease causing progressive muscle degeneration (-trophy = development)
Fracture types
Closed / Open Pathologic Comminuted (fragmented) Compression Colles (wrist) Complicated Impacted Hairline (stress) Greenstick (partial; children) Salter-Harris (growth plate)
Other trauma terms
Sprain
Ligament injury from overstretching or tearing
Strain
Muscle or tendon injury from overstretching
Dislocation
Complete displacement of bone from joint
Subluxation
Partial dislocation — bones still partially in contact
Compartment syndrome
Dangerous pressure buildup within a muscle compartment; can cause necrosis
Benign
Exostosis
Benign bony outgrowth from bone surface
Osteoma
Benign bone tumor
Chondroma
Benign cartilage tumor
Leiomyoma
Benign smooth muscle tumor (e.g. uterine fibroid)
Rhabdomyoma
Benign striated (skeletal) muscle tumor
Malignant
Osteosarcoma
Malignant bone tumor; most common primary bone cancer
Ewing sarcoma
Malignant bone or soft tissue tumor; affects children/young adults
Chondrosarcoma
Malignant cartilage tumor
Leiomyosarcoma
Malignant smooth muscle tumor
Rhabdomyosarcoma
Malignant skeletal muscle tumor; most common soft tissue sarcoma in children
Achondroplasia
Most common form of dwarfism; defective cartilage development (a- = without, chondr/o = cartilage)
Muscular dystrophy
Genetic progressive muscle wasting; several types (e.g. Duchenne)
Polydactyly
Extra fingers or toes (poly- = many, dactyl/o = fingers/toes)
Syndactyly
Fusion of two or more digits (syn- = together)
Spina bifida occulta
Hidden neural tube defect; incomplete closure of vertebral arch
Talipes
Clubfoot; congenital foot deformity
Torticollis
Twisted neck (wry neck); sternocleidomastoid muscle contracture
Diagnostic procedures
Arthrography
X-ray of a joint after contrast injection
Arthroscopy
Visual exam of a joint interior via a scope (-scopy)
CT scan
Computed tomography; cross-sectional imaging
DEXA scan
Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry; measures bone density
EMG (Electromyography)
Records electrical activity of muscles (electr/o + my/o + -graphy)
MRI
Magnetic resonance imaging; best for soft tissues
Myelogram
X-ray of spinal canal after contrast injection (myel/o = spinal cord)
Goniometry
Measurement of joint range of motion
Phalen test
Wrist flexion test to diagnose carpal tunnel syndrome
Rheumatoid factor (RF)
Blood test for RA antibodies
Serum calcium (Ca)
Blood test for bone metabolism and parathyroid function
Therapeutic procedures
Bone procedures
Osteoclasis
Surgical fracturing of a bone to correct deformity (-clasis = breaking)
Osteoplasty
Surgical repair of bone (-plasty = surgical repair)
Kyphoplasty
Minimally invasive procedure to repair vertebral compression fractures
Laminectomy
Removal of vertebral lamina to relieve spinal cord pressure
Spondylosyndesis
Spinal fusion to stabilize vertebrae (-desis = binding together)
Joint procedures
Arthrocentesis
Aspiration of fluid from a joint (-centesis = puncture)
Arthrodesis
Surgical fusion of a joint (-desis = binding)
Arthroplasty
Surgical reconstruction of a joint; includes THR, TKR
Meniscectomy
Surgical removal of the meniscus
Bunionectomy
Surgical removal of a bunion
Fracture management
Reduction (open / closed)
Realignment of fracture fragments; open = surgical, closed = manual
Fixation (internal / external)
Stabilization of fracture with hardware (pins, plates, screws)
Traction
Application of pulling force to align fractured bone
Pharmacology
Analgesics — pain relief Anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) Antirheumatics Bisphosphonates — bone density DMARDs — disease-modifying Muscle relaxants
