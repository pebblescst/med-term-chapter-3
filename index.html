Chapter 3 covers the musculoskeletal system — the most content-heavy chapter so far. Specialties: Orthopedics (orthopedist/orthopod), Rheumatology (rheumatologist), Physiatry (physiatrist — rehabilitation medicine).
5 System functions
Support
Structural framework
The skeleton provides the rigid framework maintaining body shape and supporting weight.
Protection
Organ protection
Skull → brain; vertebrae → spinal cord; ribs → heart/lungs/trachea/esophagus; pelvis → bladder/uterus.
Movement
Locomotion
Muscles attach via tendons to bones. Contraction pulls bones to produce movement at joints. Antagonistic pairs: flexor contracts while extensor relaxes.
Storage
Mineral bank
Bones store calcium (Ca) and phosphorus (P). The body makes deposits and withdrawals as needed for metabolism, nerve function, and muscle contraction.
Hematopoiesis
Blood cell formation
hemat/o + -poiesis = blood formation. Red bone marrow (myel/o) produces RBCs, WBCs, and platelets. Primarily in flat bones and proximal long bones in adults.
System components
Bones — oste/o
206 bones
Two divisions: axial skeleton (80 bones: skull + vertebrae + rib cage) and appendicular skeleton (126 bones: limbs + girdles). Shapes: long, short, flat, irregular, sesamoid.
Muscles — my/o
Three types
Skeletal (voluntary, bone-attached), smooth (involuntary, GI/vascular), cardiac (involuntary, heart). Named by location, size, shape, action, or origin/insertion.
Joints — arthr/o
3 types by mobility
Synarthroses (immovable — skull sutures), amphiarthroses (slightly movable — intervertebral discs), diarthroses/synovial (freely movable — hip, knee, shoulder).
Connective
Ligaments · Tendons · Fascia · Cartilage
Ligament (syndesm/o): bone-to-bone. Tendon (tendin/o): muscle-to-bone. Fascia (fasci/o): wraps muscles. Cartilage (chondr/o): cushions joints, flexible support. Sprain = ligament injury; Strain = tendon/muscle injury.
Key A&P terms
TermDefinition
Appendicular skeletonFree appendages and their attachments — upper/lower extremities and shoulder/pelvic girdles. 126 bones. Everything that "appends" to the axial skeleton.
ArticulationA joint (arthr/o) — where two bones meet. Range of motion (ROM) = range through which a joint can be moved. Measured in degrees with a goniometer.
Axial skeletonBones forming the vertical axis: skull + vertebral column + rib cage. 80 bones. Central support structure protecting brain, spinal cord, and thoracic organs.
Bone depressionHollow or concave area: foramen (hole/opening), fossa (pit/depression), sinus (air-filled cavity/channel).
Bone processProjection on bone surface: condyle (rounded knuckle), epicondyle (above condyle), crest (ridge), head (rounded end), spine (sharp projection), trochanter, tubercle, tuberosity.
BursaFluid-filled sac (burs/o) near a joint reducing friction between bone, tendons, and muscles. Plural: bursae. Bursitis = inflammation.
CartilageFirm, flexible connective tissue (chondr/o). Covers joint surfaces, forms structural support (ear, nose, rib tips). Not vascularized — heals slowly.
FasciaConnective tissue sheet (fasci/o) wrapping, separating, and supporting muscles/organs. Compartment syndrome = fascial compartment pressure buildup → emergency. Plantar fascia = thick band under foot.
LigamentDense connective tissue (syndesm/o) connecting bone to bone. Stabilizes joints. Sprain = ligament injury (grade I-III). Common: ACL, MCL (knee); ATFL (ankle).
MuscleContractile tissue (my/o). Three types: skeletal (voluntary), smooth (involuntary — GI, vascular), cardiac (involuntary — heart). Muscular dystrophy = progressive skeletal muscle disease.
TendonDense connective tissue cord (tendin/o) connecting muscle to bone. Strain = tendon or muscle injury. Tendinitis = inflammation. Tendons transmit muscle force to bones to produce movement.
VertebraIndividual bone of the spinal column (vertebr/o, spondyl/o). Plural: vertebrae. 26 total. Structure: spinous process (spin/o), lamina (lamin/o), vertebral body, transverse process.
Bone structure, skeleton organization, joints, and all 7 muscle action pairs.
Bone structure
Bone cells
Osteoblasts · Osteocytes · Osteoclasts
Osteoblasts: build bone (blast = builder). Active during growth and fracture repair. Osteocytes: mature bone cells in matrix. Osteoclasts: break down/resorb bone (clast = breaker). Balance between builders and breakers maintains bone density. ⚠ Osteoclast (cell) ≠ osteoclasis (procedure).
Bone types
Compact vs Cancellous
Compact (cortical): dense outer layer; strong and solid. Cancellous (spongy/trabecular): honeycomb inner structure; lightweight; contains red bone marrow (myel/o) for hematopoiesis. Matrix = calcified non-cellular calcium phosphate framework.
Long bone parts
Diaphysis · Epiphysis · Metaphysis
Diaphysis (dia- + -physis): the shaft — main long part. Epiphysis (epi- + -physis): the rounded ends. Metaphysis: flared region between shaft and ends. Epiphyseal plate: growth plate — cartilage zone where bone lengthens in children. Closes end of puberty.
Bone coverings
Periosteum · Endosteum
Periosteum (peri- + oste/o): dense fibrous membrane covering OUTER bone surface. Contains blood vessels and osteoblasts for repair. Endosteum (endo- + oste/o): thin membrane lining the INNER medullary cavity.
Axial skeleton
Skull
Cranium (8) + Facial (14)
Cranial: frontal, 2 parietal, occipital, 2 temporal, ethmoid, sphenoid. Facial: 2 zygomatic, 2 lacrimal, 2 maxilla, mandible (only moveable skull bone — TMJ), vomer, 2 palatine, 2 inferior nasal conchae.
Rib cage
12 pairs ribs + Sternum
True ribs (1-7): direct costal cartilage to sternum. False ribs (8-10): attach to rib 7. Floating (11-12): no anterior attachment. Sternum = manubrium + body + xiphoid process.
Vertebral column
26 vertebrae — 5 regions
Cervical C1-C7 (C1=atlas, C2=axis), Thoracic T1-T12, Lumbar L1-L5 (largest), Sacrum (S1-S5 fused), Coccyx. Each vertebra: spinous process (spin/o), lamina (lamin/o), body, transverse process.
Appendicular skeleton
Upper extremity
Shoulder → Fingers
Shoulder girdle: scapula (scapul/o) + clavicle (clavicul/o, cleid/o). Arm: humerus (humer/o) → radius (radi/o) + ulna (uln/o). Wrist: 8 carpals (carp/o). Hand: 5 metacarpals + 14 phalanges (phalang/o).
Lower extremity
Hip → Toes
Pelvic girdle: ilium (ili/o) ⚠ ≠ ileum + ischium (ischi/o) + pubis (pub/o). Leg: femur (femor/o — longest bone) + patella (patell/o) + tibia (tibi/o) + fibula (fibul/o). Ankle: 7 tarsals. Foot: 5 metatarsals + 14 phalanges.
Muscle actions — 7 opposite pairs
Angle at joint
Flexion
DECREASING angle at joint. Bending. Bicep curl. "FLEXing = curling = bending."
Extension
INCREASING angle at joint. Straightening. Hyperextension = beyond neutral.
Midline movement
Adduction
TOWARD midline. ADD to body. Bringing arm back to side. "ADDuction = ADD to body."
Abduction
AWAY from midline. Raising arm to side. "ABduction = ABsent from body."
Forearm/foot rotation
Supination
Palm or foot UPWARD. "Supine = face UP." Like holding a soup bowl.
Pronation
Palm or foot DOWNWARD. "Prone = face DOWN."
Ankle/foot
Dorsiflexion
Foot UPWARD toward shin. Dorsum = top of foot. Pulling toes toward nose.
Plantar flexion
Toes DOWNWARD. plant/o = sole. Standing on tiptoe. Pointing foot.
Turning out/in
Eversion
Sole faces OUT (away from midline).
Inversion
Sole faces IN (toward midline). Ankle inversion = most common ankle sprain.
Circular movement
Rotation
Bone turns on its own axis. Turning head side to side.
Circumduction
Circular movement of distal limb around attachment. Combines all movements (cone shape).
Forward/backward
Protraction
FORWARD movement (pro- = forward). Jaw or scapula forward.
Retraction
BACKWARD movement (re- = back). Jaw or scapula back. Squeezing shoulder blades.
7 prefixes and 13 suffixes. Six suffixes (-centesis, -clasis, -desis, -listhesis, -physis, -trophy) are unique to or most strongly associated with the musculoskeletal system.
Prefixes (7)

dia-through, complete, betweenDiaphysis (dia- + -physis = growing through = the shaft of a long bone); diaphragm (partition through body). Also diarrhea (Ch.5).

Suffixes (13)
SuffixMeaningExamples & notes
-alpertaining toVertebral (vertebra); costal (ribs); intervertebral; plantar (sole of foot). Universal adjective suffix.
-centesissurgical puncture to withdraw fluidArthrocentesis (joint aspiration — diagnose gout, RA, infection; inject corticosteroid). Also thoracentesis, paracentesis.
-clasisintentional breakingOsteoclasis (intentional bone breaking to correct deformity, then reset). ⚠ ≠ osteoclast (the bone-resorbing CELL).
-desissurgical binding, fusionArthrodesis (joint fusion — permanently immobilizes); spondylosyndesis (spinal fusion). -desis = permanently bound.
-graphyprocess of recording/imagingArthrography (joint imaging with contrast); electromyography (EMG = muscle electrical activity recording with needle electrodes).
-itisinflammationArthritis, osteomyelitis, bursitis, tendinitis, costochondritis. Most common pathology suffix — universal.
-listhesisslippingSpondylolisthesis (vertebra slips forward). EXCLUSIVE to spinal terminology. Greek olisthos = to slip on.
-lysisbreakdown, destruction, looseningRhabdomyolysis (skeletal muscle breakdown → myoglobin → kidney injury); ankylolysis.
-malaciasofteningOsteomalacia (bone softening); chondromalacia (cartilage softening). ⚠ ≠ -megaly (enlargement).
-physisgrowth, growingDiaphysis (shaft), epiphysis (end), metaphysis, symphysis (grown together). Anatomical/structural suffix.
-plastysurgical repair/reconstructionArthroplasty (joint replacement); osteoplasty; kyphoplasty (vertebral balloon cement repair).
-scopyprocess of visual examinationArthroscopy (visual exam of joint interior). Arthroscope = the instrument. Allows surgery through small incisions.
-trophynourishment, developmentDystrophy (abnormal development); atrophy (wasting — a- = without); hypertrophy (excessive development = muscle building).
⚠ Key look-alike pairs
-clasis vs -desis
-clasis = intentional BREAKING (osteoclasis). -desis = surgical BINDING/FUSION (arthrodesis). One breaks it; the other fuses it permanently.
-trophy vs -physis
-trophy = NOURISHMENT/DEVELOPMENT (dystrophy). -physis = GROWTH zone/structure (epiphysis, diaphysis). Both relate to growth but in different contexts.
-malacia vs -megaly
-malacia = SOFTENING (osteomalacia, chondromalacia). -megaly = ENLARGEMENT (hepatomegaly, cardiomegaly). Both start with "m" — very different pathology.
The arthr/o procedure chain
arthroCENTESIS (aspirate fluid) vs arthroDESIS (fuse joint) vs arthroPLASTY (replace joint) vs arthroSCOPY (look inside). Same root — completely different procedures based solely on the suffix.

Clinical terms organized by category. Chapter 3 has the largest clinical term count — covering congenital conditions, bone disease, arthropathies, spinal disorders, muscle disorders, trauma, and neoplasms.
Congenital conditions
Achondroplasia
a- + chondr/o + -plasia
Most common short-limb dwarfism. Without cartilage development. Arms/legs short; trunk normal. Autosomal dominant. Normal intelligence.
Muscular dystrophy
muscul/o + dys- + -trophy
Progressive skeletal muscle wasting. Duchenne MD (DMD) = most common; X-linked; males; dystrophin protein absent.
Polydactyly
poly- + dactyl/o + -y
Extra fingers or toes. poly- = many. Most common congenital hand anomaly.
Syndactyly
syn- + dactyl/o + -y
Webbed or fused digits. syn- = together. Cutaneous (skin only) or osseous (bones fused).
Spina bifida occulta
hidden vertebral arch defect
Hidden failure of vertebral arches to close. Usually asymptomatic. Occult = hidden. Worse forms: myelomeningocele.
Talipes (clubfoot)
tal/o + pes (ankle + foot)
Foot turned inward and downward. Talipes equinovarus = most common. Treatment: serial casting (Ponseti method).
Torticollis
twisted neck
Contracted neck muscles pulling head to one side. "Wry neck." Congenital or acquired. Treatment: stretching, botulinum toxin.
Bone diseases
Osteoporosis
oste/o + por/o + -osis
Porous bone — low bone density. Most common metabolic bone disease. DEXA T-score ≤ -2.5. Hip + vertebral fractures. Bisphosphonates.
Osteopenia
oste/o + -penia
Low bone mass — T-score -1.0 to -2.5. Precursor to osteoporosis. Treat: calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise.
Osteomalacia
oste/o + -malacia
Bone softening from vitamin D deficiency (adults). Rickets = same in children (bowing legs, growth plate effects).
Osteomyelitis
oste/o + myel/o + -itis
Bone marrow infection (usually Staph aureus). Hematogenous or contiguous. IV antibiotics + surgical debridement.
Osteitis deformans
oste/o + -itis
Paget disease of bone. Abnormally rapid remodeling → enlarged, deformed, weakened bones. Elevated alkaline phosphatase. Bisphosphonates.
Chondromalacia
chondr/o + -malacia
Cartilage softening. Chondromalacia patellae = softening of kneecap underside → anterior knee pain. "Runner's knee."
Arthropathies (joint disorders)
Osteoarthritis (OA/DJD)
oste/o + arthr/o + -itis
Degenerative joint disease. Cartilage breakdown → bone-on-bone. Age-related, weight-bearing joints. Morning stiffness <30 min. NSAIDs, arthroplasty.
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)
arthr/o + -itis (autoimmune)
Autoimmune synovial inflammation → symmetric small joint destruction. Morning stiffness >1 hr. RF+, anti-CCP+. DMARDs first-line.
Bursitis
burs/o + -itis
Bursa inflammation from repetitive use or trauma. Common: shoulder, hip, knee. Treatment: rest, ice, NSAIDs, corticosteroid injection.
Tendinitis
tendin/o + -itis
Tendon inflammation from overuse. Achilles, rotator cuff, patellar. Treatment: rest, PT, eccentric exercises, corticosteroid injection.
Carpal tunnel syndrome
median nerve compression
Median nerve compressed in carpal tunnel. Numbness/tingling thumb–middle fingers. Phalen test. Treatment: wrist splint, injection, surgery.
Ankylosing spondylitis
ankyl/o + spondyl/o + -itis
Inflammatory spinal arthritis → bamboo spine. Young males, HLA-B27+. Sacroiliac joints first. TNF inhibitors.
Spondylolisthesis
spondyl/o + -listhesis
Vertebra slips forward over one below. L4-L5 or L5-S1. Graded I-IV. Back pain + sciatica. PT, bracing, fusion for high grade.
Gout
urate crystal deposition
Uric acid crystals in joints → intense pain/erythema. Great toe (podagra) classic. Colchicine/NSAIDs acute; allopurinol prophylaxis.
Spinal curvatures — mnemonic
Scoliosis — Sideways
scoli/o + -osis = crooked. Lateral curve. S or C shape viewed from behind. Cobb ≥10°. Brace: 25-40°; Surgery: >40-50°.
Kyphosis — hump/posterior
kyph/o + -osis = hump. Excessive POSTERIOR thoracic curve. Hunchback. Scheuermann = structural. Kyphoplasty for compression fracture.
Lordosis — Lumbar/inward
lord/o + -osis = bent backward. Excessive ANTERIOR lumbar curve. Swayback. Common in pregnancy. L in Lordosis = Lumbar.
Muscle disorders & trauma
Rhabdomyolysis
rhabdomy/o + -lysis
Skeletal muscle breakdown → myoglobin → dark "cola" urine → kidney injury. Crush injury, extreme exercise, statins. IV fluids treatment.
Myasthenia gravis
my/o + astheni/o
Autoimmune ACh receptor attack → fatigable weakness. Worse with activity, better with rest. Ptosis/diplopia first. Pyridostigmine.
Fibromyalgia
fibr/o + my/o + -algia
Widespread musculoskeletal pain + fatigue + tender points. No structural cause. Central sensitization. Exercise, duloxetine, pregabalin.
Compartment syndrome
fascial pressure emergency
Increased pressure in fascial compartment → ischemia. EMERGENCY. 6 P's: pain, pressure, paresthesia, paralysis, pallor, pulselessness. Fasciotomy.
Sprain vs Strain
ligament vs muscle/tendon
SPRAIN = ligament injury (bone-to-bone). STRAIN = muscle or tendon injury. Both graded I (mild) to III (complete tear).
Dislocation / Subluxation
joint displacement
Dislocation = complete joint displacement. Subluxation = partial (sub- = partial). Both require reduction (realignment).
Fracture types
Closed / Open
skin intact vs penetrated
Closed (simple): skin intact. Open (compound): bone through skin → infection risk → IV antibiotics + urgent surgery.
Comminuted
multiple fragments
Bone shattered into multiple fragments. High-energy trauma. Requires surgical fixation (ORIF or external fixation).
Compression
vertebral crush
Vertebra collapses under axial load. Common in osteoporosis → kyphosis. Kyphoplasty/vertebroplasty + pain management.
Colles fracture
distal radius, FOOSH
Distal radius + dorsal displacement = "dinner fork" deformity. Fall On OutStretched Hand. Common in osteoporotic women.
Greenstick
children only
Incomplete fracture — one cortex bends, other breaks. Children only (flexible bones). Like bending a green stick.
Salter-Harris
growth plate fracture
Types I-V involving growth plate. Type I = best prognosis (slipped through). Type V = worst (crushed). Mnemonic: SALTR.
Diagnostic and therapeutic procedures for the musculoskeletal system. The suffix identifies the type of procedure.
Diagnostic procedures
TermDefinition & notes
Arthroscopyarthr/o + -scopy = visual examination of joint interior via small incision. Both diagnostic and therapeutic (meniscus repair, ACL). Knee and shoulder most common. Arthroscope = the instrument.
Arthrographyarthr/o + -graphy = imaging of a joint using contrast agent injected into joint space + X-ray or CT. Detects ligament tears, cartilage damage, loose bodies. Largely replaced by MRI.
DEXA scanDual Energy X-ray Absorptiometry. Measures bone mineral density. T-score: ≥ -1.0 = normal; -1.0 to -2.5 = osteopenia; ≤ -2.5 = osteoporosis. Screening for women ≥65.
Electromyography (EMG)electr/o + my/o + -graphy = recording electrical activity of muscles using needle electrodes. Diagnoses myopathy, neuropathy, myasthenia gravis. Often combined with nerve conduction study (NCS).
Myelogrammyel/o + -gram = imaging of spinal cord using contrast injected into subarachnoid space. Largely replaced by MRI. Still used when MRI contraindicated.
X-ray (Radiograph)First-line imaging for fractures and arthritis. Shows bone cortex, joint spaces. AP and lateral views standard. Stress fractures may be initially invisible (MRI for diagnosis).
MRIMagnetic resonance imaging. Gold standard for soft tissue: ligaments, tendons, cartilage, intervertebral discs, spinal cord, bone marrow. No radiation. Contraindicated with metallic implants.
Phalen testWrist hyperflexion held 60 seconds → reproduces carpal tunnel symptoms (numbness/tingling in median nerve distribution). Positive = CTS diagnosis. Compare: Tinel sign = tapping over carpal tunnel.
Goniometrygoni/o + -metry = measurement of joint angles using a goniometer (the instrument). Used by physical therapists to document ROM and progress in rehabilitation.
Therapeutic interventions
TermDefinition & notes
Arthrocentesisarthr/o + -centesis = surgical aspiration of joint fluid. Diagnostic: crystal analysis (gout = urate; pseudogout = CPPD), culture/sensitivity, WBC count. Therapeutic: drain excess fluid, inject corticosteroid or viscosupplementation.
Arthrodesisarthr/o + -desis = surgical fusion — permanently eliminates movement. For severely damaged arthritic joints. Common: spinal fusion (spondylosyndesis), ankle arthrodesis. Compare: arthroplasty = replaces joint.
Arthroplasty (THR/TKR)arthr/o + -plasty = surgical repair/reconstruction of a joint. Total Hip Replacement (THR) and Total Knee Replacement (TKR) = most common elective orthopedic surgeries. Metal/plastic/ceramic prosthesis (prosthes/o).
Laminectomylamin/o + -ectomy = surgical removal of vertebral lamina. Decompresses spinal canal for herniated disc or spinal stenosis. Laminotomy = smaller partial removal. Postlaminectomy syndrome = failed back surgery syndrome.
Kyphoplastykyph/o + -plasty = minimally invasive: balloon inflated to restore vertebral height → bone cement (PMMA) injected. For osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures. Vertebroplasty = same without balloon.
Spondylosyndesisspondyl/o + syn- + -desis = spinal fusion — surgically binding vertebrae together. Uses bone graft, cages, rods, screws. For: instability, herniated disc, spondylolisthesis, deformity. Also called spondylodesis or spinal fusion.
Osteoclasisoste/o + -clasis = intentional breaking of a bone to correct deformity (malunion), then reset correctly. ⚠ Not the same as osteoclast (the bone-resorbing cell).
Fasciotomyfasci/o + -tomy = incision into fascia to release compartment pressure. Emergency treatment for acute compartment syndrome. Wound left open to close later.
Fracture fixationInternal = ORIF (open reduction internal fixation) — plates, screws, rods, intramedullary nails. External = frame with pins through skin into bone. Closed reduction = manual realignment without surgery.
Pharmacology
Analgesics
Pain relief: NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen — anti-inflammatory + analgesic), acetaminophen (analgesic only), opioids (severe acute pain).
DMARDs
Disease-Modifying AntiRheumatic Drugs: methotrexate (first-line RA), hydroxychloroquine. Biologic DMARDs: adalimumab, etanercept (TNF inhibitors). Slow RA progression.
Bisphosphonates
Inhibit osteoclast activity → prevent bone resorption → increase bone density. For osteoporosis + Paget disease. Examples: alendronate (Fosamax), risedronate, zoledronic acid (IV).
Muscle relaxants
Reduce muscle spasm: cyclobenzaprine (Flexeril), baclofen, methocarbamol. For acute back pain and strain. CNS depressants — may cause drowsiness.
